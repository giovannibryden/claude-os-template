# SE Demo Scorecard

Used to evaluate demo transcripts against the FY27 demo standard (The New Mixpanel Demo Playbook). Each criterion is scored 1–5. Total possible score: 20.

---

## Rating Scale

| Score | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Exemplary | Clearly exceeds expectations. Could use as a coaching example for the team. |
| 4 | Strong | Meets expectations well. Minor gaps only. |
| 3 | Adequate | Baseline met, but inconsistently or with noticeable gaps. |
| 2 | Developing | Attempted but ineffective, or key elements missing. |
| 1 | Not present | Criterion not addressed in the demo. |

---

## Criteria

### 1. Tailoring & Preparation
**What we're looking for:** The demo feels built for this specific prospect. The SE opens with a research share that names what was learned in discovery — in the prospect's words — and confirms alignment before diving in. The agenda is shaped around 2-3 use cases tied to their stated challenges, not a generic product tour. Custom data, industry-relevant examples, and prospect-specific framing throughout.

| Score | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|
| 5 | Research share names specific challenges in prospect's words; agenda maps to those challenges; custom data and industry context throughout; zero generic sections |
| 4 | Clear tailoring throughout; research share present; mostly specific with minor generic stretches |
| 3 | Some tailoring but significant generic sections; prospect acknowledged but their problems aren't driving the agenda |
| 2 | Names dropped in but demo is largely one-size-fits-all |
| 1 | Completely generic; could be shown to any prospect |

---

### 2. Narrative & Structure
**What we're looking for:** The demo follows the Tell-Show-Tell structure from the playbook. Each use case opens by framing the business problem and where current tools fall short (Tell), demonstrates Mixpanel solving it live (Show), then closes by naming the business outcome (Tell). Anchor slides bookend each use case. Transitions between use cases connect into one story, not three separate demos.

| Score | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|
| 5 | Clear Tell-Show-Tell loops per use case; anchor slides frame and close each section; transitions between use cases feel like one story; demo stays on narrative rails |
| 4 | Tell-Show-Tell present for all use cases; minor gaps — one section missing a clean close, or transitions are abrupt |
| 3 | Structure attempted but inconsistent — some use cases follow the loop, others drift into feature tours |
| 2 | Minimal structure; mostly clicking through product without framing or closing |
| 1 | Unstructured feature tour; no discernible arc |

---

### 3. AI Narrative
**What we're looking for:** The SE delivers the AI narrative from the playbook — the three shifts (speed, autonomy, measurement), the product intelligence gap, and the Mixpanel advantage (behavioral data model as the foundation for AI). This is delivered with conviction, not recitation. The SE personalizes it with tech stack bridges (naming the prospect's current tools and where they fall short) and persona bridges (naming each person in the room and what Mixpanel unlocks for their role). AI is then woven throughout the demo as a persistent narrative, not a standalone section bolted on at the end.

| Score | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|
| 5 | All three shifts landed with conviction; product intelligence gap and Mixpanel advantage articulated; personalized with tech stack and persona bridges; AI woven throughout the demo; SE has an opinion, not just a script |
| 4 | All three shifts covered; Mixpanel advantage clear; AI well-integrated; minor gaps in personalization or conviction |
| 3 | Core message present but delivered mechanically or missing one pillar; AI shown but not positioned as a differentiator |
| 2 | AI mentioned but no clear structure; Mixpanel advantage unclear or generic; AI feels bolted on |
| 1 | AI narrative missing, skipped, or only a passing reference |

---

### 4. Business Value
**What we're looking for:** The SE acts as a consultant solving a business problem, not a product rep running a feature tour. Value is stated throughout the demo — not saved for the end. After each use case, the SE names the business outcome: what this means for the prospect's KPIs, team, or bottom line. The Tell-Show-Tell "closing Tell" is a value statement, not a feature summary. Between use cases, the SE connects what was just shown to the bigger picture before moving on. The demo closes with a clear path to value — specific next steps proposed (POC scope, technical deep-dive, follow-up session), not "we'll follow up over email."

**Scoring principle:** Score the SE's *delivery* of value framing, not whether it *landed* with the prospect. If the SE named business outcomes after each section and proposed a clear path forward, that's a 4 regardless of whether the prospect was receptive.

| Score | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|
| 5 | Consultant posture throughout; every use case closes with a named business outcome in the prospect's language; value threaded between use cases, not just at the end; specific results or ROI cited; path to value proposes a concrete next step with scope and timeline |
| 4 | Most use cases close with value; business language throughout; value present between sections; path to value with a specific next step; occasional feature framing but quickly re-anchored |
| 3 | Value mentioned but inconsistently — some sections close with outcomes, others don't; value appears at the end but not between use cases; path to value vague or AE-driven |
| 2 | Primarily feature-focused; business outcomes mentioned only at the very end or in passing; no value between sections; no clear path to value |
| 1 | Pure feature demonstration; no business outcome framing; no next steps discussed |

---

## Score Interpretation

| Total | Range | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 18–20 | Exemplary | Ready to be used as a team coaching example |
| 14–17 | Strong | Above standard; identify one area to sharpen |
| 10–13 | Adequate | Meeting baseline inconsistently; focused coaching needed |
| 6–9 | Developing | Below standard; structured improvement plan warranted |
| 4–5 | Not meeting standard | Significant coaching required; consider shadowing program |
